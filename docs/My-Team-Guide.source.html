1  What the module is

My Team is the supervisor's mirror of My Records. Each screen shows the same data as its personal counterpart, widened from one person to that supervisor's direct reports, and with the employee as the leading column.

ScreenShows, for every direct report
Team's AttendanceDaily clock-ins, clock-outs and worked hours
Team's Time EntriesA weekly roster, drillable into one person's grid
Team's LeavesEvery leave filed, with reason, notes and attachment
Team's ShiftShift change requests and where they stand

2  Who counts as “my team”

Membership is a single field: users.immediate_sup_id. A user is on your team if their immediate_sup_id is your ID. That is a direct line only — it does not walk the chain, so a report's own reports do not appear.

Enforced server-side. Every team endpoint re-derives the subordinate list from the signed-in user. Asking for a user who is not your direct report returns 403, so the filter cannot be bypassed by editing an ID in the URL.

3  Team's Attendance

One row per employee per day.

Filters cover employee, status and a date range, and the result set exports to CSV.

4  Team's Time Entries

Two views sharing one selected week.

Roster view

Every direct report for the chosen week: name, total hours and timesheet status. People who never started a sheet still appear, synthesised as not started, so the roster is a complete picture of the team rather than only those who filed.

Grid view

Clicking a person opens their week as the same read-only grid used on Time Entries — project, task, time type, memo and the seven day columns. Even an empty week renders its day headers, so the layout does not collapse.

A Previous / Next stepper moves the week, with the week label shown beside it.

Weeks are Monday-based. Dates are formatted in the browser's own timezone rather than UTC — converting first would roll local midnight back to Sunday, and because the API snaps to the Monday of the week, that would silently land on the previous week.

5  Team's Leaves

A table of every leave filed by the team: employee, leave type, start, end, duration, reason, notes, attachment, submitted and status. A Status dropdown filters the list and carries live counts, and the date range uses the same overlap test as Leave Requests.

  • Reason and Notes are clamped to two lines, with the full text on hover.
  • An attachment opens in a preview modal — images inline, PDFs in a frame, with Download as a fallback.
  • The list is read-only. Approving happens on the Dashboard.

6  Team's Shift

Shift change requests from the team: employee, current shift, requested shift, effective date, reason, notes, submitted and status.

One gap to know about. The status dropdown offers All, Pending, Approved and Rejected. The database also stores applied — a request whose effective date has passed. Those rows are only visible under All Statuses.

7  Where approval actually happens

None of these four screens approve anything. They are the record; the decision is made in the Requests panel on the Dashboard, which is the single approval queue for leaves, shift changes and timesheets alike. The team screens are where a supervisor looks things up, filters and exports.

My Team — review→ Dashboard — decide→ Status updates everywhere

Permission is necessary, not sufficient. Holding view team attendance does not reveal the whole company — the result is still filtered to your own direct reports. A Manager with nobody reporting to them sees four empty screens.
