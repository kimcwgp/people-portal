1  What the module is

Time Entries is a weekly timesheet, modelled on NetSuite. Instead of logging each day separately, you fill one grid per week: every row is a piece of work, and the seven columns are Monday to Sunday.

A week always runs Monday to Sunday. Any date you open resolves back to that week's Monday, so there is exactly one timesheet per person per week — enforced by a unique constraint on (user_id, week_start).

Two screens, one dataset. Time Entries under My Records is where you file your own time. Team's Time Entries under My Team is where a supervisor reviews their direct reports. Both read the same timesheets table; only the vantage point differs.

2  Time Entries — filing your own week

The weekly list

The landing view lists recent weeks — by default the last 8 weeks up to the current one. Weeks you have never opened still appear, synthesised on the fly with a zero total, so a week you forgot is visible rather than missing. Clicking any row opens that week's grid.

3  The approval lifecycle

Draft→ Pending→ Approved ↳ Rejected → editable again
StatusEditable?What it means
draftYesCreated the moment you first open a week. Yours to edit freely.
pendingNoSubmitted and locked, waiting on your supervisor.
approvedNoSigned off. Permanently locked; the approver and timestamp are recorded.
rejectedYesSent back with a note. Unlocked so you can fix it and resubmit.

Submitting

  • A week needs at least one line — submitting an empty grid is refused with "Add at least one line before submitting."
  • The approver is set automatically to your immediate supervisor.
  • Any previous rejection note is cleared, so a resubmitted week arrives clean.

What locking actually enforces

Editing is guarded server-side, not just hidden in the UI. Every write checks two things:

  • The timesheet belongs to you — otherwise 403 That timesheet is not yours.
  • The status is draft or rejected — otherwise 422 A pending timesheet cannot be edited.
Why rejection unlocks rather than deletes. Sending a week back sets it to rejected and stores the reason. The grid shows the note at the top, the owner fixes the lines, and resubmitting puts it straight back in the approver's queue. Nothing is retyped.

4  Team's Time Entries — the supervisor view

The personal screen answers "which of my weeks are done?" — one person, many weeks. A supervisor needs the opposite, so this screen inverts the axes: the week is the filter and the people are the rows.

The roster

Pick a week with Previous / Next, optionally search by name, and you get one row per direct report:

EmployeeTotal HoursLinesApproval Status
Divina Reyes0:000NOT STARTED
Enrico Santos0:000NOT STARTED
Team Lead User24:544DRAFT
People who have filed nothing still appear, as not started. A query that returned only existing timesheets would hide exactly the people a supervisor most needs to chase. The summary line counts only pending and approved as "submitted", so a draft never reads as done.

A summary strip above the table gives the week at a glance: how many team members, how many have submitted, and total hours logged.

Drill-down

Clicking a row opens that person's week in the same grid as the personal screen — same day headers, same column totals, same coloured Time Type chips — but read only. The week stepper carries across both views, so you can walk one person back through several weeks and return to the roster on whichever week you landed on.

Export

Export CSV downloads the selected week for the whole team: one line per timesheet row, plus a row for anyone who has not filed, so the file matches what is on screen. Columns are Employee, Email, Week, Status, Project/Task, Ticket, Time Type, Memo, Mon–Sun and Row Total.

Scope

A supervisor sees direct reports only — users whose immediate_sup_id is the signed-in user. It is not recursive: a manager does not see their team leads' teams. Opening a week for someone who is not your direct report returns 403 You can only view the time entries of your direct reports.

5  Approving and sending back

Approvals happen on the Dashboard, not here. Team's Time Entries is deliberately read only, so there is one place where approvals are actioned rather than two.

Pending timesheets from your direct reports appear in the Dashboard Requests row, in a card labelled Time Entries. Opening it shows the pending list; picking one shows the full week grid — the actual lines being signed off, not just a total — with Approve and Reject beneath it.

  • Approve locks the week, records you as approver with a timestamp.
  • Reject requires a reason. Without one you get "Please provide a reason for rejection." The week returns to the owner, editable, with your note displayed.
  • Approving twice is refused: "This timesheet is already approved."

6  Data model

Three tables. All use soft deletes.

time_types

The lookup HR curates. name, tint (chip colour), description, is_billable, is_active, sort_order.

timesheets

One row per person per week.

ColumnNotes
user_id, week_startUnique together — one sheet per person per week
week_endAlways the Sunday of that week
statusdraft / pending / approved / rejected
submitted_atSet on submit
approver_id, approved_atWho signed off and when
rejection_noteThe reason a week was sent back; cleared on resubmit
timesheet_entries

The grid rows.

ColumnNotes
timesheet_idParent week
project_id, project_ticketOptional project link and free-text reference
time_type_idFK to time_types
memoShort description
mon_hours … sun_hoursSeven decimal(5,2) columns, one per day
sort_orderRow order within the week

Totals are computed, never stored: a row total is the sum of its seven day columns, a week total is the sum of its rows, and a daily total is the sum of one column across rows.
