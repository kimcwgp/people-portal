1  What the module is

The Dashboard is the landing page after sign-in and does two jobs at once. For every associate it is the time clock — where the working day is started, paused and ended. For anyone with direct reports it is also the approval inbox, collecting everything waiting on their decision.

One request builds it: GET /api/user/dashboard returns four blocks — attendance, requests, stats and announcements.

2  The time clock

A single button toggles between TIME IN and TIME OUT, posting to /api/user/dashboard/clock. The server decides which action applies from the state of today's attendance row, so a double-click cannot produce two clock-ins.

OUT→ IN→ ON BREAK→ IN→ OUT
  • Clocking in creates the day's attendances row and flips users.online to true; clocking out flips it back.
  • Clocking out a second time returns 409 Already clocked out for today rather than silently overwriting the first time out.
  • Optional notes ride along with either action and are stored on the attendance row.
  • check-auto-timeout exists for shifts that end without anyone pressing the button; it closes the row rather than leaving it open forever.
Times are datetimes, not times. time_in and time_out hold a full timestamp. A night shift that ends after midnight therefore records correctly instead of appearing to end before it started.

3  Breaks

Breaks are rows in attendance_breaks hanging off the day's attendance. Two types are configured, both deducted from worked hours:

TypeDefault allowanceEnvironment key
Lunch60 minutesLUNCH_BREAK_MINUTES
Be right back30 minutesBRB_BREAK_MINUTES

Starting a break posts to /breaks/start with the type; ending it posts to /breaks/end. The response carries the remaining allowance for that type so the page can show how much is left without a second round-trip. Going over the allowance is recorded, not blocked — the overage simply reduces worked hours.

4  Leave credits

An Available Leave Credits card shows the year-to-date balance for PTO, VL, SL, BDO and CTO. These are a yearly figure and are deliberately unaffected by anything else on the page. The same five tiles appear on Leave Requests, reading the same leave_credits row, so the two screens can never disagree.

5  The Requests queue

The Requests panel is populated only for users who have direct reports. It reads directSubordinates and returns an empty list immediately if there are none, so an ordinary associate never pays for the query.

Request typeSource
LeavePending leaves rows belonging to direct reports
Shift changePending shift_change_requests rows
TimesheetPending timesheets, with the full week grid attached
Direct reports only. The queue follows users.immediate_sup_id, not the team hierarchy. A manager two levels up does not see a request unless it is escalated to them by reassignment.

6  Approving from the dashboard

Each row opens a modal carrying enough context to decide without leaving the page. Timesheets are the richest case: the modal renders the whole week as a read-only grid — project, task, time type and the seven day columns with their totals — so the approver reviews the actual lines rather than a single hours figure.

PENDING→ APPROVED PENDING→ REJECTED

Rejection requires a note. The note is written to the request and surfaces to the author on their own screen, so a sent-back item always says why.

7  Announcements

The announcement strip shows the most recent active HR announcements, authored in HR Announcements. It is read-only here.

9  Permissions

Every route above sits behind the single permission view dashboard, held by all six roles. Authority to approve is not a permission — it comes from being the requester's immediate_sup_id. Someone with view dashboard and no reports sees the clock and nothing else.
